## Background

SoilCheck answers one question each morning: **which pots need checking today?** You add your plants once — what they are, what they're growing in, indoor or out — and the app works out the rest.

It's designed to be read on a phone, on the patio, in bright sun, with dirty hands. That constraint drove almost every decision.

## The Model

Each plant gets a drying interval:

```
interval      = baseDryingDays × containerMultiplier × weatherFactor
nextCheckDate = max(lastWatered, lastChecked) + interval
urgency       = (now − lastEvent) / interval
```

Terracotta breathes, so it dries at `0.7×`. A hanging basket loses water on every side at `0.5×`. A self-watering pot holds out at `2.0×`.

<Callout>
**Urgency is a fraction of the interval, not days overdue.**
A basil two days late is a genuine emergency; a snake plant two days late is not. Cards sort by that fraction, and a moisture column down each card's edge draws it directly — full after watering, draining as the soil dries, rust once overdue. It reads at arm's length without parsing a number.
</Callout>

## What I Built

- **Watered vs. still moist** — two deliberately different actions. Both reset the schedule, but only one claims you watered, so the history stays honest.
- **Rain prompts** — the app checks National Weather Service observations near your ZIP and *asks* whether your pots got wet. It never decides on its own.
- **Temperature adjustment** — hot weather ahead shortens outdoor intervals and a cool spell lengthens them, averaged over three days of forecast highs so one hot afternoon doesn't whipsaw the garden.
- **Installable and offline** — a PWA with an app-icon badge showing how many pots are due, plus export/import backups.

## Key Technical Decision

<Callout>
**Ask about rain instead of acting on it.**
NWS precipitation data is sparse — stations report rain when it falls and omit the field otherwise. Across five stations near Wilmington, NC, nearby stations disagreed on rainfall amounts by up to 3×, while agreeing on *whether* it rained. That's fatal for an automatic adjustment and fine for a prompt a human confirms. Temperature, which every station reports on every observation, is the one input the app trusts without asking.
</Callout>
